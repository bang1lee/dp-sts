# Gujora / Yundol — procedural particle film

This version decodes the accepted no-people cover and turns its bright line pixels into seeded 3D particle chains. It samples the actual source geometry, then renders the points in space; it does not display a texture-mapped image plane. Inverse perspective reconstructs the original composition at rest. The scene contains a low, broad island with a steep left shoulder and long right slope, a separate band of water, a foreground beach, seven individually constructed people, a small cylindrical brazier, fire, smoke, sky particles and a few landward pieces of driftwood. Its approximate geometry is a visual interpretation, not a geographic survey. Branches are restricted to the dry landward portion of the beach, away from the waterline.

A perspective camera makes a small arc through real three-dimensional point coordinates. The island and curved shoreline derive from actual source pixels, with a small number of neighboring points joined by short filaments. Particles at different scales receive slow coherent three-axis motion, with increased dispersal during the middle section and a return toward their original positions. Water chains are interleaved in three past, present, and future time slices with small depth offsets during dispersal; smoke has similarly offset paths. These are visual metaphors, not a physical model. The three-minute score is: 0–30 seconds establish the scene, 30–85 living motion, 85–135 smoke and wider dispersal, 135–165 a luminous canopy and returning light, and 165–180 settling. This is an intended direction until the rendered frames and sequence have been reviewed.

The figures are three men near the fire, a separate man and woman, and a separate mother and child. Each is built from head, torso and limb point volumes. The brazier wall has height 0.12 world units, one quarter of the 0.48-unit adult height. The smoke rises through depth and gradually opens into sky points; the returning light is symbolic and has no personified deity, face or lettering. The palette is predominantly cool silver on black with a small warm fire accent.

The audio input is `audio.wav` and matching `audio-features.json` under `--input`. Band envelopes are smoothed over 1.2 seconds from the beginning of the audio, including for partial previews; there is no beat-dependent visual trigger. The WAV SHA-256 must match the analysis. The final default input is the newly prepared ambient WAV and matching analysis in `runs/dp-sts-gujora-particles-v01`.

Default full export: `node tools/gujora-particles.mjs --input=PATH --out=PATH`. Twelve-second preview at 72 seconds: add `--preview`. `--start=SECONDS` and `--duration=SECONDS` set a frame-aligned interval; `--still-times=15,72,110,150` selects frames in that interval; `--stills-only` skips video encoding and renders only selected times. The default `--cover` path points to the accepted no-people source PNG and can be overridden with `--cover=PATH`. Its SHA-256 is recorded in the output metadata. The full render is 1080 × 1080, 24 fps, H.264/AAC, 180 seconds. Output video, metadata, and selected frames are protected from overwrites. On success the render records actual frame count, input hashes, errors, timing, geometry details, and unmodified renderer and scene source snapshots and their hashes.

Geometry and runtime syntax can be checked without rendering. Visual readability, audio fit, GPU compatibility and render speed require a real preview; a preview is not a completed film.
