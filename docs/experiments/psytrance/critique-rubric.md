## Decision rule

PASS requires all hard gates, at least 80/100 overall, and no category below 3/5. Ratings: 1 absent/broken, 2 weak, 3 serviceable, 4 convincing, 5 distinctive and resolved. A score is an editorial judgment with timestamped evidence, not a computed substitute for perception. Unassessed categories remain UNASSESSED, never automatically awarded points. Unresolved major concerns mean REVISE. If a sensory channel is unavailable, the verdict must state that limitation and cannot claim full audiovisual artistic approval.

## Hard gates

- Deliver an actual full-duration stereo audio master and playable synchronized MP4, approximately ten minutes (9–11 minutes is the working tolerance unless the user specifies otherwise).
- The actual rendered sound must establish psytrance identity, and actual sampled frames must visibly be black/white dotted fractals. Labels, code, planned scenes, or green tests cannot establish either.
- No accidental silence, dead/blank stretches, corruption, unintended discontinuities, or missing ending. Intentional silence and darkness need demonstrable compositional purpose.
- No sustained broad full-screen flashing presented as dot shimmer; the visual rhythm must retain form and negative space. Assess comfort editorially rather than claiming formal safety certification.
- Full-file technical analysis and whole-timeline evidence must accompany direct inspection of representative output. A polished poster or short pilot does not approve the ten-minute work.
- A repeatability claim requires actual comparable runs and matching recorded evidence for the delivered version. Artistic approval and reproducibility are separate judgments.

## Inspection plan

1. Record exact paths, durations, codecs, dimensions, sample rates, revision identifiers and output hashes. Bind every observation to the actual delivered revision.
2. Survey the whole timeline with contact sheets and temporal audio/brightness/density measures. Seek unplanned plateaus, repeated imagery, dead zones and unmotivated changes. These measures locate problems; they do not prove artistic quality.
3. Inspect opening, every major section, every transition, peak, and final resolution. Include the center of the longest steady groove, not only selected highlights. At least eight 20–40 second windows should cover the structure; use additional fine samples around suspected defects.
4. Inspect closely spaced frame sequences for dot motion, shimmer, spatial hierarchy and transitions. Still contact sheets cannot prove motion quality; explicitly distinguish still-derived conclusions from motion inspection.
5. Listen to the actual audio wherever supported, including kick/bass detail, a breakdown-to-return transition, the loudest peak and final tail. If direct audition is unavailable, document the precise limit, perform signal checks, and leave auditory aesthetic judgment unassessed. Playback invoked on the host alone is not proof the critic heard it.
6. Compare audio onset/phrase evidence with frame changes at beginning, middle and end. Confirm offset and drift are bounded by one output frame where discrete accent timing is intended. Deliberately smoothed modulation can lag; label it and judge the perceptual result rather than pretending every feature must have zero latency.
7. Report observations by timestamp: what happens, why it weakens or strengthens the brief, required change, and verification needed. Separate blockers from preferences. Do not request cosmetic churn after acceptance criteria are met.
